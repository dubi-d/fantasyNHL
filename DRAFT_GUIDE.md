# Draft Guide

For a daily-lineup, head-to-head categories league with multiple D slots,
one goalie slot, and G/A/STP/SOG/HIT/BLK/DEF/SV/GAA scoring. Reconsider the
strategy if those settings change.

**Use your league-adjusted projections as the draft board. Early: buy
hard-to-replace production. Later: balance the team.**

## Where to spend picks

| Stage | Priority |
|---|---|
| Early | Elite forward offense and offensive defensemen |
| Middle | Remaining offensive value, complementary players, discounted goalie |
| Late | Category gaps, role upside, useful opening schedules |

- **Defense:** aim for one or two strong offensive D before their tier disappears.
  Two in the first five rounds is a starting plan, not a quota. Pay for
  A/STP/DEF and offensive opportunity—not primarily HIT/BLK.
- **Forwards:** keep buying G/A/STP/SOG through the middle rounds. Don't switch
  to peripheral specialists just to complete category coverage.
- **Goalies:** be patient, but take value when it falls. No mandatory round,
  no chasing runs. Consider expected starts as well as quality; more starts
  help SV but can hurt GAA. Waiting requires active management afterward.
- **Hits/blocks:** usually cheaper later or through pickups. Delay their cost,
  not necessarily the categories themselves.

## Balanced versus specialized

**Balance the roster, not every player.**

- Early, broad contributors preserve flexibility—especially players combining
  strong offense with meaningful peripherals.
- Later, favor whoever improves your actual weaknesses. More production in a
  category you already dominate may do little for matchup wins.
- Don't sacrifice scarce offense merely to make a player's profile look even.
- Equally, don't dismiss a balanced player: supplying offense and peripherals
  together saves roster spots otherwise spent on specialists.

For a balanced D versus an offensive specialist, compare **the offensive gap,
your HIT/BLK needs, and the cost of replacing either package**. Neither style
deserves an automatic bonus.

## Adjust to your draft slot

Assuming a 14-team snake draft:

| Position | Approach |
|---|---|
| Early: 1–4 | Elite anchor; look for offensive D around the round 2–3 turn |
| Middle: 5–10 | Stay flexible; react to tiers disappearing rather than force positions |
| Late: 11–14 | Build a premium pair: F+D or F+F depending on value; reassess D at the next turn |

Near either end, plan paired picks together. Before a long wait, ask:
**Which player has the weakest likely replacement at my next pick?**

Use projections for value and platform rankings/ADP for likely availability.
Draft position changes timing, not your fundamental priorities.

## Schedule tiebreakers

- **Core players:** consider regular-season and playoff schedules.
- **Replaceable late picks:** prioritize the opening matchups.
- Check each playoff round; avoid stacking the same weak round.
- Off nights matter when they prevent benching. NHL team games are not goalie starts.
- Don't cross a substantial projection gap for schedule or multiply projections
  by schedule scores. Refresh schedules and verify playoff dates each year.

## Lessons to carry forward

- Review what required early investment versus what was available later—not
  just last year's biggest names or final standings.
- Separate decision quality from hindsight. Injuries and breakouts don't alone
  prove good or bad drafting; full-season stats aren't necessarily points earned
  while you owned the player.
- Historical scarcity is a clue, not a law. Undrafted players weren't necessarily
  available whenever needed, and pickups cost roster spots and acquisitions.
- Draft a core, not permanent commitments. Every late pick should offer a clear
  category contribution, upside, or immediate usefulness. Be willing to move on.

## Close-pick checklist

1. Are projected value and availability genuinely similar?
2. Which category/position package is harder to replace?
3. Does it complement my roster or inflate an existing surplus?
4. Who is likely to survive until my next pick?
5. If still tied, which schedule suits my intended holding period?

**Don't double-count balance or scarcity if your ranking already values it.**
